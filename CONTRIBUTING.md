# Contributing to the RedForce AI Documentation Repository

---

## Purpose of This Repository

This repository contains the public-facing documentation for RedForce AI — an enterprise AI security validation platform. Its contents include vision documents, problem statements, design principles, use cases, and reference material intended for security leaders, enterprise architects, AI researchers, and the broader AI security community.

The source code for RedForce AI is maintained separately and is not part of this repository. Contributions here are limited to documentation.

---

## What Contributions Are Welcome

We welcome contributions that improve the clarity, accuracy, and completeness of the documentation. Specifically:

- **Corrections** to factual errors, outdated standard references, or inaccurate technical claims
- **Clarity improvements** that make complex concepts more accessible to the target audience without sacrificing precision
- **Gap identification** — flagging topics that are absent from the documentation but are relevant to enterprise AI security practitioners
- **Standards alignment** — updates that reflect newly published or revised versions of OWASP, NIST, MITRE, ISO/IEC, or applicable regulatory frameworks
- **Research contributions** — additions grounded in published, peer-reviewed, or authoritative industry research that strengthen the evidential basis of the documentation

Contributions that introduce marketing language, speculative claims, vendor comparisons, or implementation-specific detail that belongs in the private codebase are outside the scope of this repository.

---

## Reporting Documentation Issues

If you identify an error, inconsistency, or gap in the documentation, please open an issue in this repository. A useful issue report includes:

- The document and section where the issue appears
- A clear description of what is incorrect, unclear, or missing
- Where relevant, a reference to the authoritative source that supports the correction

Issues that lack sufficient context to act on may be closed pending clarification. We aim to acknowledge all substantive issues within a reasonable timeframe.

---

## Suggesting Improvements

Suggestions for new documentation, structural reorganisation, or scope expansion are welcome as issues. When proposing a significant addition, please include:

- The audience the proposed content is intended for
- The gap it addresses in the current documentation
- A brief outline of the proposed content and its evidential basis

Suggestions will be reviewed by the maintainers against the repository's scope and the documentation standards described below.

---

## Documentation Style Guidelines

All documentation in this repository is written for a professional audience that includes CISOs, enterprise architects, AI security researchers, and compliance professionals. Contributors should apply the following standards:

- **Clarity over brevity, precision over simplicity.** The audience is technically and professionally capable. Oversimplification that sacrifices accuracy is not an improvement.
- **Active voice and direct statements.** Avoid passive constructions and hedging language where a direct statement is accurate and defensible.
- **No marketing language.** Superlatives, comparative claims, and promotional phrasing are not appropriate in technical and policy documentation.
- **Consistent terminology.** Use terms consistently with their definitions in the OWASP, NIST, and MITRE frameworks. Where a term is used in a specific sense, define it on first use.
- **Section and heading hygiene.** Headings should describe content accurately. Avoid headings that are clever at the expense of being informative.

---

## Principles for Technical Accuracy and Evidence-Based Writing

Documentation in this repository makes claims about the AI security threat landscape, the limitations of existing tools, and the requirements of enterprise security practice. These claims must be defensible.

- **Cite authoritative sources.** Claims about vulnerability classes, attack techniques, or standards requirements should reference published sources — OWASP, MITRE ATLAS, NIST publications, academic research, or recognised industry frameworks.
- **Do not state as fact what is opinion.** Where a position reflects a design philosophy or editorial judgement rather than an established fact, frame it accordingly.
- **Standards references must be current.** References to OWASP Top 10, LLM Top 10, API Top 10, Mobile Top 10, and equivalent frameworks must cite the current active version. Outdated version references will be corrected during review.
- **Do not overstate coverage or capability.** Documentation that implies broader scope or stronger guarantees than the platform currently provides will not be accepted.

---

## Code of Professional Conduct

Contributors to this repository are expected to engage with the same professional standards that govern the AI security community at large.

- Engage with other contributors respectfully and in good faith.
- Ground disagreements in evidence and reasoning, not assertion or authority.
- Acknowledge when a correction or improvement suggested by another contributor is valid.
- Disclose any conflict of interest relevant to a proposed contribution.
- Do not submit contributions that misrepresent research, plagiarise existing work, or attribute claims to sources that do not support them.

Contributors who do not meet these standards may have their contributions declined and their access to the repository reviewed.

---

*Thank you for contributing to the quality and integrity of the RedForce AI documentation. This repository is a public-facing representation of the platform's standards — contributions that uphold those standards are valued.*
